After going through all three missions, a suitable route for a robot sharing space with people should be efficient and reliable. For example, in mission 1, I learned that finding the shortest path does not always mean a robot will navigate efficiently. Using BFS and A* as a comparison, both found the shortest route of 13.5 m on the open map, but A* expanded 700 nodes compared to BFS's 928 nodes. In mission 2, I learned that a planned route does not guarantee successful navigation. Using the constrained map, BFS successfully reached the goal in 90.452 seconds, while DFS timed out after approximately 179.934 seconds. Finally, in mission 3, I learned that a robot should also consider people around it. Using A* with social_2 increased the minimum person clearance from 0.7253 m to 0.7621 m and reduced time spent inside personal space from 1.6 seconds to 0 seconds. Overall, I learned that a good route is not just the shortest one. It should also be practical for real life use.
