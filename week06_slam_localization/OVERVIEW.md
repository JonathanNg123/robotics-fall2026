# Week 6 Lab Overview — SLAM and Localization

This individual ROS 2 lab connects motion estimates, sensor evidence, maps, and uncertainty. Students operate established SLAM Toolbox and AMCL systems; they do not implement scan matching or particle filtering. The shared course container from Week 1 supplies ROS 2 Jazzy, Gazebo, TurtleBot3, RViz, and the course tools.

Five short guided tutorials prepare the investigations. Tutorial 1 recalls encoders, differential-drive motion, dead reckoning, and accumulated odometry error from earlier labs. Tutorial 2 compares topological and metric maps and shows how a LiDAR ray contributes evidence to an occupancy grid. After preflight, Tutorial 3 teaches students to distinguish a live scan, an estimated pose, and accumulated map evidence in RViz. Between mapping and localization, Tutorial 4 contrasts building a map with localizing in a fixed saved map. Tutorial 5 introduces AMCL particles, competing pose hypotheses, and the possibility of a concentrated but incorrect belief.

Mission 1 asks students to save a route prediction before mapping, teleoperate a TurtleBot3, revisit a mapped region, and save a validated YAML/PGM/analysis bundle plus RViz evidence. They interpret coverage, isolated occupied cells, map-boundary contact, and visible structure; they investigate whether route, limited sensing, or accumulated pose error could explain a defect. The composite score is a discussion aid, not ground truth.

Mission 2 changes the exploration strategy while holding the world, robot, map settings, and approximate duration fixed. Students save a prediction, record experimental controls, map again, and compare maps side by side using quantitative and visual evidence. They distinguish evidence of correction to existing structure from mere map growth and acknowledge confounding differences.

Mission 3 uses one selected map for four clean-start AMCL trials: good initial pose, incorrect initial pose, ambiguous location, and degraded scan. Students predict and record each trial separately, inspect pose and covariance time series, particle-cloud behavior, and scan agreement, then compare all four outcomes. The recorder can compute position/heading error and false-confidence periods only if a separately verified reference pose in the map frame is available. Without that source, students make an explicitly qualitative correctness judgment; covariance must never be presented as physical error. Students apply and critique a small evidence-only slow/stop/ask-for-help rule and connect degraded sensing to Lab 5.

The final 200–300-word technical synthesis explains what it means for the robot to “know where it is” using motion, sensor, map, and localization evidence. A separate individual reflection is 1–300 words. The submission includes checked artifacts and a hashed manifest; recoverable autosaves, explicit navigation, and a ZIP backup protect work across restarts and updates.
